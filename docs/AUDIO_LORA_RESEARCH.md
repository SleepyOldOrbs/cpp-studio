# Audio LoRA for comedy-show production

Verified: 2026-08-14

Planning decision: the proposed Corpus Preparation Workspace is deliberately
limited to Performer–Character speech. Applause, laughter, Foley, ambience, and
other non-speech sounds will continue to use cpp-studio's existing Stable Audio
3 Medium/Small generation rather than entering this annotation, corpus, or LoRA
workflow. The Stable Audio LoRA information below remains research context, not
selected product scope.

- **Yes, there is a real speech LoRA equivalent.** VoxCPM2's official repository supports LoRA and full fine-tuning for speaker, language, or domain adaptation, says 5–10 minutes of paired speech can be enough to start, and includes a training WebUI. Training needs clean audio with accurate transcripts, not unlabelled mixed episodes. Sources: [VoxCPM](https://github.com/OpenBMB/VoxCPM), [official trainer](https://github.com/OpenBMB/VoxCPM/blob/main/scripts/train_voxcpm_finetune.py), [VoxCPM2 model card](https://huggingface.co/openbmb/VoxCPM2).

- **Stable Audio 3 has an actual audio LoRA, but it is for music/SFX rather than dialogue.** Its official guide asks for captioned audio (at least roughly 20–50 clips) and produces a small adapter; its model overview explicitly says it is not designed for speech or voice. It could learn a licensed show's non-speech identity—room, audience, stings, transitions, recurring effects—but not intelligible cast performances. Sources: [LoRA guide](https://github.com/Stability-AI/stable-audio-3/blob/main/docs/workflows/lora.md), [model limits](https://github.com/Stability-AI/stable-audio-3/blob/main/docs/guides/model-overview.md).

- **Reference conditioning may beat training for the first experiment.** Qwen3-TTS Base advertises three-second voice cloning, while Fish Audio S2 advertises 10–30-second cloning, multi-speaker/multi-turn generation, and controls such as pauses, chuckles, interruption, and audience laughter. Qwen's official fine-tuning is currently single-speaker SFT, not LoRA; Fish S2's weights are non-commercial/research unless separately licensed. Sources: [Qwen3-TTS](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS), [Qwen fine-tuning](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS/blob/main/finetuning/README.md), [Fish S2](https://github.com/fishaudio/fish-speech), [Fish licence](https://github.com/fishaudio/fish-speech/blob/main/LICENSE).

- **A whole broadcast episode is poor voice-training data.** Multiple speakers, overlap, music, audience, compression, and room sound become unwanted training signal. The practical dataset is speaker-separated, clean clips with corrected transcripts, trained per consenting voice; non-speech clips and captions belong in a separate Stable Audio LoRA.

- **The crucial limit: audio training does not learn why the comedy works.** A speech LoRA performs supplied text; an SFX LoRA creates sound. Premise, setup/payoff, character relationships, escalation, callbacks, and dialogue rhythm belong in the script generator or writing process. A separate text-LLM LoRA/SFT can learn conversational patterns from licensed scripts; Qwen/Hugging Face tooling supports LoRA/QLoRA and conversational datasets. Sources: [Qwen3 QLoRA guide](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3/blob/main/docs/source/training/axolotl.md), [TRL conversational SFT](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/en/sft_trainer).

- **The local engine already has one relevant adapter seam.** The sibling `audio.cpp` source at local release-0.5 commit `3178daf` can load VibeVoice fine-tune adapter directories via `vibevoice.lora`, merging the LM LoRA and overlaying the diffusion head/connectors. This is adapter loading, not a trainer; official Microsoft LoRA documentation currently covers VibeVoice ASR, while TTS trainers are community/experimental. Local evidence: `../audio.cpp/docs/tts.md`, `../audio.cpp/src/models/vibevoice/lora.cpp`, and `../audio.cpp/model_specs_v1/vibevoice.json`. The current local engine does not expose VoxCPM2 LoRA loading, so using an official VoxCPM2 adapter in cpp-studio would need a separate integration.

- **Rights and consent are real gates.** The UK Government states that copying copyright works for AI training requires a licence unless an exception applies; owning broadcasts does not itself grant training rights. Its March 2026 report also says current law does not cover every unauthorised digital voice replica. Voice consent is separate from programme copyright: for example, ElevenLabs only permits a verified Professional Voice Clone of the account holder's own voice. Sources: [UK copyright-and-AI position](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/copyright-and-artificial-intelligence/copyright-and-artificial-intelligence), [2026 UK report](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/report-and-impact-assessment-on-copyright-and-artificial-intelligence/report-on-copyright-and-artificial-intelligence), [ElevenLabs consent rule](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/product/voice-customization/voice-cloning/can-i-create-a-professional-voice-clone-of-someone-elses-voice). This is a factual boundary, not legal advice.

- **K.I.S.S. recommendation:** test one short original scene with original voice designs or authorised clean references; compare zero-shot performance with one small VoxCPM2 LoRA for a single voice; optionally train a separate Stable Audio 3 LoRA for licensed ambience/SFX. Judge script quality, performance, and sound design separately before building an end-to-end trainer.
