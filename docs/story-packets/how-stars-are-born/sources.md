# Sources: how stars are born

This packet is the hand-authored product seed for the first factual story
fixture. URLs are attribution metadata for V1; the gateway does not fetch them.

## src-1: NASA Science - Star Basics

URL: https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/

Facts to use:

- Stars form in large molecular clouds made of gas and dust.
- Molecular clouds can be very massive and can span many light-years.
- The clouds are cold, which helps gas clump into denser pockets.
- As clumps gain mass, gravity strengthens until some clumps collapse.

## src-2: NASA Science - Webb Catches Fiery Hourglass as New Star Forms

URL: https://science.nasa.gov/missions/webb/nasas-webb-catches-fiery-hourglass-as-new-star-forms/

Facts to use:

- A forming protostar gathers material from its surrounding molecular cloud.
- Falling material spirals inward and forms an accretion disk.
- The disk feeds material onto the protostar.
- As mass gathers, the protostar compresses and moves closer to stable nuclear
  fusion.

## src-3: NASA Science - Hubble's Album of Planet-Forming Disks

URL: https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/hubbles-album-of-planet-forming-disks/

Facts to use:

- Stars form from collapsing clouds of gas and dust.
- Some falling material forms a rotating disk around the protostar.
- Jets released from magnetic poles are part of star formation.
- Jets help carry away angular momentum so material can keep collecting.

## src-4: ESA - Webb unveils young stars across every stage of formation

URL: https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2026/06/Webb_unveils_young_stars_across_every_stage_of_formation

Facts to use:

- Molecular clouds are dense clumps of gas compared with the surrounding
  interstellar medium.
- Dense molecular clouds can collapse under gravity and form stars.
- The earliest stage is a protostar.
- A protostar is a growing star fed by gas from the cloud through a spinning
  disk.
